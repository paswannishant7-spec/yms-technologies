Why One Search

Three kinds of lookup. One place to start.

A railway journey starts with stations and a date. A flight search starts with cities or airports. A vehicle lookup starts with a registration number. One Search keeps those different inputs in their own sections while giving visitors a single place to choose the right path.

The design keeps the same visual language across categories. Orange identifies the main search action, white cards group related information, and labelled shortcuts let someone jump to the part of the app they need. This page lets you see that structure before entering any information into an app.

Railway

Plan a railway journey.

Start with a departure station, destination and journey date. Explore a sample train card, then see how a timetable or station detail could organise the next layer of information.

The Railway search design gives the route and date their own fields. That makes the starting point clear whether you know both stations already or are still working out which station serves the place you want. The entry screen also groups shortcuts separately from the form, so someone looking for a particular train or station has another way in.

Open Train results to see a separate lookup by train name or number. Its sample cards place the train identifier, endpoints and operating days together. The Train schedule screen then switches from a summary to a station-by-station timetable. Station details frames the same information around one station instead of one train, which is useful when the station is the part of the journey you know first.

Flight

Look up flights by route.

Choose the origin, destination and date. The design shows how an airline route finder could group direct and one-stop options and present a route in more detail.

The Flight entry view keeps the familiar origin, destination and date pattern. The wording and visual cues shift to air travel, but the starting question stays the same. This is particularly handy when you are exploring the same trip by different modes and want to see what information each search asks for.

The Route results prototype groups direct and one-stop routes. In the Route details prototype, each card adds sample cities, dates, aircraft and operating days. Airport and airline names, times, counts and status labels in the artwork are examples from the design board. Use the gallery to inspect the hierarchy and compare views at their full proportions.

RTO

Find vehicle information.

Start with vehicle services, then open the dedicated lookup to search by vehicle number. This preview uses an empty recent-searches state.

The Vehicle section changes the search input from a place or date to a registration number. The overview groups the lookup with other vehicle-service shortcuts, while the dedicated Vehicle Search screen gives it its own heading, single input and recent-searches area. The empty state shows where a user would start before making a lookup.

The separate vehicle-services and Traffic Rules cards in the gallery show how supporting topics fit into the proposed navigation. Those cards are interface previews. The public showcase uses the search and discovery screens, with no vehicle-owner names, personal records or identifiable search results.

Connected experience

Switch categories. Keep your bearings.

The home design brings service shortcuts together. Search pages also use a shared layout, with a label for the current section followed by the fields and a clear action. Look for these elements as you switch from a railway lookup to a flight route or a vehicle search.

The screens illustrate different information layouts. A timetable has a row for each stop, a route finder groups options, and the vehicle search starts with a single field. The consistent navigation makes each of those layouts easier to recognise.

One Search FAQ

Questions about the app.

What is One Search?

One Search is a YMS Technologies product that brings railway, flight and vehicle information categories into one mobile interface. The gallery shows the supplied designs and app references for those categories.

Can I run a search from this page?

The controls on this page change interface previews. Select a category and then choose a screen to see the next view. The example numbers, dates and route records are design content.

What can I explore in the Railway and Flight previews?

Railway includes a route entry view, sample train cards, a timetable and station details. Flight includes an entry view, route results and route details. The screen labels identify the examples so you can trace the different parts of each design.

Why are there two vehicle screens?

The services overview shows the selection of vehicle-related topics. Vehicle lookup shows the dedicated registration-number interface and its empty recent-searches state.

Where can I download the app?

A verified store listing is still needed before this site can link to a download. For a question about availability, use One Search Support.

Where can I get help?

Open One Search Support and identify the feature and step where you need help. Include the app version and device model if you know them. Keep vehicle numbers, identity documents and passwords out of your message.

Privacy & support
